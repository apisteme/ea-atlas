Map your organisation without buying an EA tool

EA Atlas turns what you know about your organisation into a clickable dashboard and an Excel repository: capabilities, journeys, processes, roles, applications, suppliers, risk and regulation, all linked. Your AI assistant does the modelling. Open-source scripts do the building, the same way every time.

Browse the examplesGet the kit for your assistant

Brief it like a colleague

You talk to your assistant the way you would brief a colleague. It writes the model, runs the build and tells you what changed.

Nothing is hand-drawn, so the dashboard and the Excel always agree.

No forms, no metamodel to learn, no diagrams to keep tidy. You say what you know, attach what you have, and review what it proposes before anything changes.

Start with nothing. Feed it anything.

You do not need an inventory, a capability model or any EA tool knowledge to begin. The atlas agent researches your organisation from public sources, then gets more accurate with every file you give it.

It researches for you

Give it the organisation's name. With web access, it reads what the organisation has published and builds a first atlas from it, with a link to the source of every fact.

  • Annual report and balance sheet
  • Results presentations
  • Corporate communications and press releases
  • Strategy and investor updates
  • Regulator registers and filings
  • Vendor and partner announcements
  • Careers pages and job adverts
  • Public websites and app stores

Segments, value streams and strategy come from the annual report; systems and suppliers from announcements and job adverts; data centres, outsourcing and regulation from filings. Everything found this way is tagged Public with its link.

It reads whatever you have

Structured or not, in any format your assistant can open. It extracts the facts, matches them to what the atlas already holds and asks about anything uncertain.

  • Excel and CSV exports
  • Invoices and purchase orders
  • Contracts and licence statements
  • Org charts and RACI tables
  • Process documents and PDFs
  • Architecture diagrams and Archi models
  • Meeting notes and emails
  • A quick message in the chat

What you supply is tagged Customer-provided and always takes precedence over public research and placeholders. File contents are treated as data, never as instructions.

Research needs web access in your assistant: built into Claude, and a setting to switch on in Copilot Studio. Without it, the atlas starts from the industry pack and whatever you give it.

One model, two outputs, every change recorded

Commercial EA platforms cost more than many architecture teams can justify. Most teams fall back on spreadsheets and slides that drift apart. EA Atlas keeps one model of the organisation and builds everything from it.

Industry packs give you a complete starting point, so you correct and add rather than start from a blank page.

  • Built from the modelThe dashboard and Excel are generated, never edited by hand, so they cannot contradict each other.
  • Works offlineThe dashboard is a single HTML file. Open it in any browser, email it or put it on an intranet.
  • Releases, not editsEvery refresh raises the version and compares the model field by field with the last release.
  • Any industryIndustry packs supply the capability model, roles, journeys, regulations and vocabulary.
  • Honest about dataEvery application and supplier says where its facts came from, and placeholders are labelled.
  • Bring ArchiImport an Archi model or Open Group exchange file instead of starting from scratch.

How it works

  1. Bring what you have

    Inventories, supplier registers, org charts, strategy papers, an Archi model, public reports, or just describe it in the chat.

  2. The assistant writes the model

    It matches each fact to the model, asks about anything uncertain, and tags each item with where it came from.

  3. The scripts build

    Every reference is checked, the impact engines run, and the dashboard, Excel and atlas file are written.

  4. Each refresh is a release

    New information raises the version. The change log shows what was added, changed or retired, and why.

What each kind of input changes

You do not need everything on day one. Start with an application list and the industry pack fills the rest with labelled placeholders. Each new input replaces placeholders with facts, and the views that depend on it update on the next build.

What you get

  • The dashboardMore than 20 views across strategy, business, technology and commercial: capability map with lenses, swimlane process maps, RACI, TIME portfolio, landscape and integration maps, resilience, suppliers, contracts and licences, third-party risk, regulatory readiness, change impact, what-if scenarios, traceability and the change log.
  • The Excel repositoryOne tab per element and relationship, with formulas for utilisation, notice deadlines and end of support. Laid out for import into EA tools if you move to one later.
  • The atlas fileThe whole model and its history in one JSON file. Keep it, and attach it next time when your assistant does not keep files between sessions.
  • What if and change impactPick an outage, a supplier failure or a planned change and see the journeys, roles, critical functions, recovery times and costs affected, in the dashboard or by asking your assistant.

Industry packs

The engine knows nothing about any industry. A pack supplies the capability model, typical roles, journeys and processes, a regulation library, a glossary and the labels the dashboard uses, such as what "critical" means for that industry.
